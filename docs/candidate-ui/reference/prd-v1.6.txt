JobPunter Product Requirements Document (PRD) + Technical Spec
AI-Powered Evidence-Based Job Search & Application Platform
Status: MVP / Product DefinitionProduct type: Candidate-agnostic job search, matching and application management platformPrimary principle: AI interprets. Evidence supports. Rules decide. The candidate remains in control.

Note: This document is not static. More functionalities will be added as the system grows. Hence, version number mentions or version number misalignment should not be reason to question the requirements. However, the higher version number should always be regarded as most recent

Part A — Product Requirements Document
1. Problem
Job seekers face several connected problems:
Job boards produce large volumes of opportunities with little meaningful fit assessment.
Keyword matching cannot reliably distinguish genuine qualification from superficial keyword overlap.
Candidates waste time applying to jobs that conflict with their experience, seniority, domain, location, work authorization, preferences, or other constraints.
Existing CVs are often disconnected from the candidate’s complete career evidence.
Tailoring a CV for each opportunity is repetitive and can introduce exaggerated or invented claims.
Job alerts arrive through multiple channels and repeatedly surface the same opportunities.
Discovery, evaluation, application preparation, and application tracking are fragmented.
Application pipelines often lack clear ownership, status transitions, reasons, and audit history.
AI-generated career materials can introduce unsupported claims.
Candidates may need different AI providers, models, and access arrangements over time.
Candidates need control over factual information and final application decisions.
1.1 Core problem
The problem is not simply finding jobs. It is finding relevant opportunities, deciding whether applying is rational based on actual evidence and constraints, preparing accurate application materials, and managing the resulting process without wasting effort or losing information.
2. Product solution
JobPunter is a candidate-agnostic, evidence-based job search and application platform. It maintains a structured Master Career Profile as the factual source of truth and evaluates real job opportunities against that evidence.
The platform must:
Build and maintain a structured career profile.
Discover real job opportunities from configured sources.
Retrieve and validate actual job descriptions where possible.
Normalize and deduplicate opportunities.
Preserve source provenance and discovery history.
Extract and classify job requirements.
Compare requirements against candidate evidence.
Separate core-role fit from domain fit and other dimensions.
Identify hard blockers, uncertainty, and evidence gaps.
Produce explainable APPLY, REVIEW, WATCH, or REJECT recommendations.
Help prepare accurate application materials grounded in approved evidence.
Track applications through a controlled and auditable lifecycle.
Support user-configured discovery schedules.
Prevent repeated discovery from creating duplicate canonical jobs.
Support multiple AI providers and models without making the product dependent on one provider.
Remain deployable independently of Google AI Studio.
3. Product principles
3.1 Evidence over keywords
Matching must rely primarily on candidate evidence and semantic interpretation, not keyword frequency.
3.2 Candidate-agnostic design
The product must support different professions, industries, seniority levels, and career paths. No production workflow may assume that a candidate is a Product Manager, software engineer, healthcare professional, or any other particular profession.
3.3 Candidate facts are authoritative
The Master Career Profile and approved Evidence Library are the authoritative sources for candidate claims.
AI may interpret, organize, summarize, and suggest. It must not silently invent achievements, metrics, employment history, qualifications, responsibilities, project outcomes, or other facts.
3.4 Deterministic rules control hard constraints
Eligibility, authorization, explicit exclusions, workflow state, deduplication, freshness, access control, and other suitable rules must be enforced by deterministic application logic.
AI must not override a deterministic hard blocker.
3.5 Human approval remains central
The candidate controls whether to pursue an opportunity, approves generated application materials, records real-world application events, overrides recommendations, and accepts or rejects offers.
Automatic application submission is outside the MVP.
3.6 Explainability
Every material recommendation must explain the relevant requirements, evidence, constraints, blockers, gaps, uncertainty, confidence, and source information.
3.7 Provenance and auditability
The product must preserve the origin and history of important jobs, evidence, evaluations, configuration changes, generated documents, and application events.
3.8 Provider independence
No core business logic may depend on a single AI provider or provider-specific SDK.
3.9 Production truthfulness
Production discovery must use real opportunities from real sources. Simulated data may exist in isolated test or demo environments, but it must never be presented as real production discovery.
3.10 MVP discipline
Once the complete core workflow is functional, major feature development pauses while the product is tested with real candidates. Further development must be guided by evidence of user demand and product performance.

9. Evidence Library and Evidence Health
The Evidence Library provides a consolidated, traceable view of candidate evidence.
It must support browsing, editing, source attribution, classification, verification status, conflict detection, missing-information identification, and evidence-health review.
Each evidence item should retain its source and relationships to relevant profile items, work experience, projects, imported documents, or other evidence.
Imported or extracted information must not silently overwrite existing facts. Conflicts must be surfaced for candidate review.
10. Document Import
Supported MVP formats:
PDF
DOCX
TXT
The import flow must support file selection, format validation, upload status, parsing status, extracted content, preview, conflict identification, source attribution, and candidate confirmation.
Required flow:
UPLOAD → VALIDATE → PARSE → EXTRACT → PREVIEW → IDENTIFY CONFLICTS → CANDIDATE CONFIRMATION → SAVE
Imported information becomes a candidate for inclusion in the profile or Evidence Library. It does not become authoritative merely because extraction succeeded.
11. CV Studio
11.1 Master CV
The Master CV is generated from the Master Career Profile. It must be fully viewable, editable, saveable, regenerable, and downloadable.
The Master Profile remains the factual source of truth.
11.2 CV variants
A CV variant is an approved presentation of the same underlying evidence for a particular pathway.
It should retain the pathway, target role family, target domain, evidence emphasis, terminology preferences, selected experience and projects, and approval status.
Variants must be fully reviewable and editable. Editing a variant must not modify the Master Career Profile.
12. Job Discovery
JobPunter must discover real job opportunities, not generate hypothetical openings and present them as live jobs.
Discovery modes remain:
Quick Match: user-initiated immediate discovery and evaluation.
Scheduled Daily Match: user-configured daily run and time.
Scheduled Weekly Match: user-configured day and time.
Manual URL: user submits a job-posting URL.
Manual JD: user pastes job-description text when a URL is inaccessible or unavailable.
Manual Email: user provides supported job-alert or recruiter-message content.
Automated Email Alerts: supported email alerts are processed through an explicitly authorized integration.
Permitted APIs and feeds: where access terms and technical conditions permit.
All channels feed the same canonical acquisition pipeline. A source being listed as a possible source does not mean that it is currently accessible or automatable.

14. Canonical Job Pool
The Job Pool is the canonical shared repository of job opportunities. It is not a separate repository for every candidate and must not be duplicated merely because multiple candidates discover or evaluate the same underlying vacancy.
A canonical job should retain, where known:
Title and employer
Location and remote status
Employment type
Salary, if supplied by the source
Seniority
Job description and requirements
Responsibilities
Application URL
Source and provenance references
External job ID

Discovered, first-seen, last-seen, and last-verified timestamps
JD retrieval status
Freshness and closure status
Verification status
Content hash or fingerprint
Relevant version history
Unknown information must remain unknown. JobPunter must not infer a missing salary, location, remote status, or requirement and present the inference as source fact.
Candidate evaluations, preferences, evidence, application materials, and application history remain private to the candidate.
15. Job Ingestion and Normalization
All acquisition channels must feed a common pipeline:
SOURCE → ACQUISITION → RAW SOURCE RECORD → SPLIT INDIVIDUAL JOBS → NORMALIZE → RESOLVE IDENTITY → RETRIEVE/VALIDATE JD → CHECK COMPLETENESS → DEDUPLICATE → RECORD PROVENANCE → UPDATE FRESHNESS → CANONICAL JOB POOL → CANDIDATE EVALUATION
The implementation must preserve the raw source record or a sufficient immutable snapshot to support audit, reprocessing, and investigation of material discrepancies.
15.1 Source acquisition and raw records
The system must separate:
The source and its access state
The acquisition attempt
The raw result or message
The individual job record extracted from that result
The canonical Job
The candidate-specific evaluation
A source search result is a discovery signal, not canonical truth. It must be verified and normalized before it is treated as a canonical opportunity.
15.2 Job description retrieval and completeness
Track explicit JD states, including:
FULL
PARTIAL
UNAVAILABLE
URL_INACCESSIBLE
REQUIRES_REVIEW
CLOSED
STALE
Completeness must be assessed deterministically against required fields and material job information. Missing critical information must reduce confidence or require review. An incomplete JD must not receive a falsely confident evaluation.
If URL retrieval fails, the candidate should be offered manual JD entry where appropriate. A failed retrieval must not make the opportunity silently disappear.
15.3 Normalization and identity resolution
Normalize title, employer, location, job type, application URL, and other available fields without inventing missing facts.
Resolve duplicate discoveries using available signals, such as:
External source ID
Canonical application URL
Normalized employer and title
Location
Normalized JD fingerprint
Content hash
Other deterministic identifiers
Ambiguous matches must not be merged solely on a weak similarity signal. The system must retain enough identity-resolution evidence to investigate false merges and missed duplicates.
15.4 Idempotency and convergence
Repeated ingestion of the same source item must converge on the same underlying canonical record where identity can be established.
Repeated processing must not create duplicate jobs, duplicate discovery history, duplicate applications, or unintended repeated notifications.
When an existing job is rediscovered:
Retain its canonical identity and application history.
Record the additional discovery event.
Update last-seen and relevant verification information.
Preserve source provenance.
Record material changes and content versions.
Re-evaluate when a material change justifies it.
Avoid reintroducing an unchanged rejected job as if it were new.
15.5 Freshness and closure
Track posting date when known, discovered date, first seen, last seen, last JD retrieval, last evaluation, last verification, stale status, and closure status.
A stale or closed job must not be presented as a fresh opportunity. Freshness is distinct from discovery: seeing a job again does not prove it is still open.

17. Decision Engine and Punt Qualification
The evaluation engine retains the four decisions:
APPLY
REVIEW
WATCH
REJECT
These decisions describe the evaluation result. They do not, by themselves, describe application lifecycle state.
17.1 Decision meanings
APPLY: evidence and constraints indicate a sufficiently strong opportunity to recommend pursuing.
REVIEW: the opportunity may be viable but needs human judgment because of uncertainty, incomplete evidence, or an important trade-off.
WATCH: the opportunity may be relevant but is not currently ready for pursuit, for example because the JD is incomplete, freshness is uncertain, the application route is unresolved, or the candidate may need to review or strengthen evidence.
REJECT: a material blocker or sufficiently weak fit makes application effort inefficient under the current constraints.
17.2 Punt terminology
The product vocabulary remains:
Job Pool: the broader pool of opportunities.
Punt: a qualifying opportunity that JobPunter considers worth the candidate’s application effort.
Applied: a Punt for which the candidate has actually submitted an application.
Landed: a successful job outcome.
Core progression: Job Pool → Punt → Apply → Land
A Punt is not a rejection. It is an opportunity worth taking a calculated shot at based on the product’s assessment. The user still decides whether to apply. A Punt is also not an Application: application state must be recorded separately and must reflect actual candidate-controlled events.
17.3 Evaluation output
Every evaluation must produce a structured, explainable record containing, at minimum:
Decision and overall assessment
Core-role, domain, technical, seniority, education, location/work authorization, preference, accessibility, and freshness assessments
Supporting candidate evidence
Hard blockers
Evidence gaps
Requirements not met
Requirements requiring verification
Confidence and explanation
Evaluation timestamp
Source and job-version references
AI provider/model/task/configuration metadata when AI was used
The UI must clearly distinguish “no evidence found” from “evidence indicates the candidate does not have this capability.”
18. Application Route

Application cost and accessibility can affect recommendations according to candidate preferences. JobPunter must not imply that an application route is free, usable, or currently open unless that has been verified to the extent possible.
19. Review Queue

It may include:

Incomplete job descriptions
Uncertain or conflicting evidence
Unclear application routes
Candidate overrides
Newly discovered APPLY recommendations requiring attention
The queue must preserve the canonical job identity and candidate-specific evaluation.

Master Career Profile + Approved CV Variant + Actual Job Description + Approved Evidence
The source CV variant must remain unchanged. The tailored document must not introduce unsupported facts or claims.
20.2 Cover letter
The cover letter must be specific to the opportunity and use supported candidate evidence only.
20.3 Application questions and answers
For likely application questions, provide:
Question
Suggested answer
Evidence used
Confidence

Requirements not addressed
Unsupported claims or unresolved warnings
Generation metadata

A later regeneration must not silently replace the historical record of a previously approved package.
21. Document Generation
The user workflow remains:
GENERATE → FULL PREVIEW → EDIT → SAVE → DOWNLOAD → REGENERATE
Supported outputs should include DOCX and PDF. Generated files must be genuinely formatted documents, not plain text renamed with a document extension.
The system must retain the relationship between a generated file, its package version, the source content, and the approval state.
22. Application Pipeline
The application lifecycle remains:
APPROVED TO APPLY → APPLIED → SCREENING → INTERVIEW INVITED → INTERVIEW → OFFER → WON
Alternative terminal or side outcomes include LOST and ARCHIVED.
The current state and next valid action must be clear to the candidate.
22.1 Ownership of events
The system may record its own verified actions, such as:
Job discovered
JD retrieved
Evaluation completed
Package generated
Package approved
The candidate controls real-world application events, including:
Applied
Screening
Interview invitation
Interview
Offer received
Offer accepted or rejected
Lost
Archived
The system must never claim an application was submitted, an interview occurred, or an offer was received without an actual recorded event supported by the candidate or a verified integration.
22.2 Application audit
Every material transition must record previous state, new state, timestamp, actor, reason, and notes where applicable.
Actors include USER, SYSTEM, and INTEGRATION.
“Mark as Applied” must actually change the application state and create an audit event. User overrides must preserve the original decision and must not rewrite the underlying evidence.
22.3 Lost and won outcomes
Lost reasons may include failure to receive an interview invitation, rejection after screening or interview, candidate withdrawal, position filled, job closure, compensation mismatch, location/work authorization, or another specified reason.
Won outcomes include offer acceptance or hiring. Offer received, accepted, and rejected should remain distinguishable events or states.
23. Scheduled Job Matching
Candidates may configure daily or weekly matching.
Settings include enabled/disabled state, time or day, selected pathways, selected sources, and search constraints.
Each run must:
Search configured sources whose state permits the requested operation.
Record source-level outcomes.
Ingest and normalize results.
Deduplicate and resolve identity.
Retrieve or update job descriptions.
Validate completeness and freshness.
Evaluate relevant new or materially changed opportunities.
Suppress unchanged duplicates.
Update the canonical Job Pool and candidate-specific evaluations.
Record run history, errors, retries, and partial results.
Notify or display results according to configured preferences.
A source failure must not be represented as a successful empty search. Partial source failure must be visible.
24. AI Provider, Model, and Task Management
AI tasks must be provider-neutral and configuration-driven.
Supported conceptual tasks include document parsing, profile extraction, evidence extraction, JD extraction, requirement classification, semantic matching, pathway discovery, CV generation, cover-letter generation, application Q&A, and evaluation reasoning.
Provider-specific SDKs must remain inside adapters. The application must distinguish consumer AI subscriptions from API access and must not scrape or automate consumer ChatGPT or Claude accounts.
Model configuration should support provider, model, task compatibility, access method, availability, cost classification, context capability, structured-output capability, tool capability, configured status, preferred model, fallback model, and task-specific routing.
Use inexpensive capable models for normalization, classification, simple extraction, and duplicate detection where suitable. Use stronger models for difficult semantic matching, transferability analysis, pathway reasoning, and complex document generation. Cache appropriate repeat operations and avoid repeatedly sending unchanged candidate/JD information to expensive models.
25. Human Overrides

New decision

Reason
Notes
An override changes the candidate’s chosen disposition, not the underlying factual evidence. A user may explicitly change a preference or constraint, but the system must preserve that change as a separate fact/configuration event rather than silently treating an override as proof that the original constraint never existed.

27. Security, Privacy, and Candidate Control
The product must:
Protect provider API credentials and privileged integration credentials.
Keep secrets out of frontend code and browser storage.
Separate provider credentials from candidate data.
Minimize sensitive information stored and transmitted.
Preserve clear data ownership boundaries.
Enforce candidate data isolation.
Preserve provenance and audit history.
Avoid sending unnecessary candidate data to AI providers.
Require explicit authorization for integrations.
Clearly distinguish Google sign-in from permission to access Gmail, Drive, Sheets, Calendar, or other services.
Keep application submission under candidate control in the MVP.
Ensure that administrative settings cannot bypass authentication, authorization, privacy controls, or deterministic hard constraints.
28. MVP Acceptance Criteria
The existing acceptance criteria remain in force.
Profile
A candidate can create and edit a profile, enter structured information, import documents, review extracted information, resolve conflicts, configure preferences, and define or approve pathways.
CV
A candidate can generate, view, edit, save, regenerate, and download a Master CV and CV variants without modifying the factual Master Profile unintentionally.
Jobs
A candidate can run quick matching, configure scheduled matching, ingest URLs and supported email/JD content, see source provenance, retrieve JDs, evaluate opportunities, see explainable decisions, avoid duplicates, and identify stale or closed jobs.
Production results must come from real acquisition or user-supplied source content. The system must identify failed, restricted, unavailable, and partial source results truthfully.
Applications
A candidate can choose an opportunity, generate and download a tailored CV, generate a cover letter and application answers, edit all materials, approve a package, mark an application as submitted, move it through the pipeline, record outcomes, and inspect audit history.
AI
The system can use multiple providers and models, route tasks to different models, use fallbacks where configured, validate output, detect unsupported claims, and keep provider credentials secure.
Candidate neutrality
The system works for different professions and for new or empty profiles. No production logic assumes a specific profession.
Admin and system control
An authorized administrator can manage supported system configuration, validate and publish configuration versions, roll back to a prior valid version without deleting history, inspect audit events, and trace AI executions to the task/configuration/model version used. Invalid configuration cannot become active, and administrative configuration cannot override core security or deterministic hard constraints.

30. Post-MVP Gate
Once the complete workflow is functional:
Stop major feature development.
Test with real candidates and measure whether JobPunter reduces wasted applications, identifies relevant jobs, improves application preparation, saves meaningful time, produces trustworthy recommendations, and earns repeat use.
Only then determine the next phase:
MVP → Technical Hardening → Private Beta → Real-User Validation → V2
Potential V2 capabilities should be selected from observed user demand and product evidence rather than assumed in advance.

Part B — Technical Specification



33. Purpose and relationship to the PRD
This Technical Specification translates the PRD into an implementable architecture. It defines system boundaries, persistence, identity and authorization, data structures, job acquisition, normalization, matching, AI orchestration, document generation, application lifecycle, integrations, scheduling, security, auditability, deployment, failure handling, and technical acceptance criteria.
The PRD remains the authority for product behavior. The Technical Specification defines how that behavior should be implemented.
Where the v1.2 technical defaults conflict with the revised v1.3 architecture, the v1.3 decisions in this section control. Product requirements not explicitly superseded remain in force.
34. Architecture principles
The following principles are mandatory:
Evidence over keywords: matching evaluates actual candidate evidence.
Candidate facts are authoritative: the Master Career Profile and approved Evidence Library are the source of truth for candidate claims.
Deterministic rules control hard constraints: AI does not override hard eligibility or security enforcement.
Human approval: the candidate makes application decisions and approves application materials.
Explainability: recommendations expose supporting evidence, blockers, gaps, and uncertainty.
Provenance: jobs, candidate evidence, imported documents, AI evaluations, generated materials, configuration, and application events retain source relationships.
Idempotency: repeated ingestion and processing converge without unintended duplicate records or events.
Provider independence: core business logic is not tied to one AI provider.
Candidate isolation: private candidate information is protected from other users.
Deployment portability: JobPunter must not depend on permanent hosting inside Google AI Studio.
Server authority: the server enforces authentication-dependent authorization, validation, state transitions, and privileged business mutations.
Database authority: durable production application state lives in the authoritative database, not in browser state.
Source truthfulness: source and integration status reflects observed state and known limitations.
Configuration governance: administrative configuration is validated, versioned, auditable, and traceable to execution.

The target architecture is a portable web application supported by a Python API and a relational database.

39. Identity, authentication, and authorization
39.1 Identity-provider boundary
Identity must be handled through a replaceable identity-provider integration. The domain model must not depend on provider-specific identity fields beyond the minimum external identity reference needed to associate an authenticated principal with a JobPunter account.
The final identity provider and registration methods remain unresolved.
Once selected, the provider must be integrated with the backend’s trusted authentication and authorization boundary. The backend must validate authenticated requests and associate each request with a stable JobPunter account identity.
39.2 Account model
A JobPunter account should retain, at minimum:
Internal user/account ID
External identity-provider reference
Display name and email where supplied
Photo URL where available
Account status
Onboarding status
Created and updated timestamps
Last-login information where available and appropriate
Authentication identity must not be duplicated unnecessarily across application entities.
39.3 Authorization
The backend must enforce ownership before returning or modifying private data.
Private candidate resources include:
Master Career Profile
Preferences and constraints
Career pathways
Evidence and imported documents
CV variants
Candidate evaluations
Application packages and artifacts
Applications and application events
Private integration settings and credentials
Candidate activity records
The canonical Job Pool may be shared across candidates. Candidate-specific evaluations and application records must remain private.
Administrative access requires a separate authorization boundary. Hiding an Admin tab or route in the frontend is not authorization.
39.4 Google service authorization
Signing in through Google, if selected, does not automatically grant access to Gmail, Drive, Sheets, Calendar, or other Google services.
Each integration requires explicit authorization, appropriate scopes, secure credential handling, clear connection state, and a revocation path.

41. File and artifact storage
Uploaded documents and generated DOCX/PDF files should be stored in object storage behind a provider-neutral storage interface.
The relational database retains metadata and relationships, including owner, artifact type, content type, storage reference, creation time, source entity, package version where applicable, and relevant processing state.
Storage must enforce private access. Public, guessable URLs must not be used to expose candidate files. Where temporary download links are used, they must be scoped and time-limited as appropriate.
The final storage vendor remains unresolved.
42. Source Registry and connector contract
First class source connectors: jobspy, scrapy, playwright.
Each automated source connector must implement a shared internal contract so the acquisition pipeline does not depend on one job board’s API, HTML layout, or response structure.
A connector should define operations equivalent to:
Validate configuration and required credentials
Check source health or access state
Acquire a bounded batch of results
Return raw source records with provenance
Report pagination and continuation state where supported
Report rate limits, restrictions, partial failures, and retry guidance where available
Normalize source-specific identifiers into common acquisition metadata
Expose supported capabilities and limitations
Support controlled shutdown or disabling
The common acquisition service owns canonicalization, deduplication, persistence, provenance, and downstream processing. A connector must not bypass these stages by writing directly to candidate evaluations or application records.
42.1 Source policy and permitted access
Automated acquisition must respect the access method and conditions applicable to each source. The architecture must not assume that all job boards permit unrestricted scraping or that a public page can be harvested at arbitrary scale.
For each source, implementation planning must establish:
Whether access is permitted
Whether an official API/feed exists
Whether authentication or a commercial agreement is required
Whether automated access is technically feasible
What rate or pagination limits apply
What data may be retained
How source failures or restrictions are reported
When automation is not available or appropriate, the product should retain supported alternatives such as manual URL ingestion, manual JD entry, or user-provided alert content.
42.2 Connector failure handling
A connector failure must not be converted into an empty successful result set.
The acquisition run must retain source-specific outcomes, including successful, partial, restricted, failed, and unavailable states as applicable. Retries must be bounded and must respect known source limits.
The Admin Control Plane must be able to show source configuration and observed operational state without falsely implying successful acquisition.
43. Canonical ingestion pipeline
The ingestion pipeline must be independent of the channel that discovered the opportunity.
Required stages:
Resolve the configured source and acquisition method.
Execute the acquisition attempt.
Persist acquisition metadata and the raw source record or sufficient source snapshot.
Split multi-job messages or result pages into individual job candidates.
Normalize available fields.
Resolve source IDs, URLs, and identity signals.
Retrieve the actual JD where possible.
Validate content completeness and identify missing critical fields.
Resolve the candidate job’s identity against existing canonical jobs.
Create or update the canonical job and relevant version history.
Record discovery provenance and source timestamps.
Update freshness and closure status.
Queue eligible downstream extraction/evaluation work.
Record completion, partial success, or failure.
The order may vary internally where needed for technical reasons, but the invariants must hold: source evidence is preserved, duplicate handling is safe, incomplete data is not misrepresented, and candidate-specific evaluation remains separate from shared canonical job data.
44. Acquisition idempotency, retries, and concurrency
44.1 Idempotency
Each acquisition operation should use stable source identifiers and/or idempotency keys where available. Replaying a source record must not create duplicate canonical jobs or duplicate logical discovery events.
Idempotency must also apply to:
Background work retries
Scheduled run retries
AI execution retries where results may be persisted
Document generation requests
Package creation and versioning
Notification dispatch
Application state changes
44.2 Concurrency
Concurrent discovery of the same vacancy from multiple sources must converge safely. Database constraints, transactions, and controlled identity-resolution logic must protect against races that could create duplicate canonical jobs.
44.3 Retries and terminal failures
Retryable failures must be distinguished from permanent or policy-based failures. Each work item must have observable state, attempt count, timing, and an error category that is safe to expose operationally.
A failed work item must not silently disappear. Reprocessing must be possible where appropriate without duplicating durable outcomes.
45. Job description completeness and freshness
JD completeness must be checked deterministically against defined minimum requirements for meaningful evaluation. The exact required-field threshold is a configuration/implementation detail to be validated; it must not be set so loosely that a title and employer alone are treated as a complete job description.
At minimum, evaluation must know whether the following are present, absent, or unknown when relevant:
Role title and employer
Job description or responsibilities
Requirements
Location or remote status
Application route
Material eligibility information
A missing field is not automatically proof of disqualification. The decision engine must distinguish unknown information from a known blocker.
Freshness and closure must be maintained independently of matching score. The system must not infer that a job is open solely because a source record remains in storage.

The backend must separate deterministic filters from AI-assisted semantic interpretation.

Use AI for:

Transferability analysis
Career pathway reasoning
Document drafting
Nuanced classification
Explanations grounded in structured evidence
AI output must be schema-validated and semantically checked before it can affect persisted domain state. A well-formed response is not automatically a correct response.
46.3 Evaluation snapshots
A candidate evaluation must be traceable to the job content, candidate evidence, candidate preferences/constraints, rule configuration, and AI task versions used at evaluation time.
When material inputs change, the system must be able to determine whether an evaluation is stale and whether reevaluation is warranted. An evaluation must not be silently rewritten as if it had been generated from information that was not available at the time.
47. AI orchestration and provider adapters
AI is a replaceable service capability, not the business architecture.
The execution path is:
AI TASK → ORCHESTRATOR → TASK/MODEL SELECTION → PROVIDER ADAPTER → MODEL → STRUCTURED RESPONSE → VALIDATION → DOMAIN LOGIC
The orchestrator must support multiple providers and models, task-specific routing, fallbacks where configured, output validation, execution tracing, cost/latency tracking where available, and controlled failure handling.
Provider-specific SDKs and response formats must be isolated inside adapters. Core services should consume normalized internal request and response contracts.
Provider credentials must be held server-side or in an appropriate secret-management mechanism. They must not be exposed in frontend bundles, browser storage, public API responses, or logs.
48. Canonical AI Task Registry and execution contract
Every production AI operation must execute through the canonical AI Task Registry.
The contract is:
AITask ID → Published AITaskVersion → System Policy → Task Instructions → Input Schema → Runtime Context → Output Schema → Validation → Business Logic
No production AI execution path may bypass this contract.
48.1 Task definition
Each task must define, as applicable:

48.2 System policy and task instructions
System policy governs global behavior, evidence-grounding requirements, prohibited behavior, safety boundaries, and non-negotiable rules.
Task instructions define the specific task’s objective, procedure, context interpretation, and expected behavior within the system policy.
Neither is allowed to override deterministic business logic or security enforcement.
48.3 Output validation
The system must validate:

Contradictions

Domain-specific invariants
Consistency with deterministic hard constraints
Invalid or incomplete output must be rejected, retried under controlled rules, or routed for review. It must not be silently accepted because it parses as JSON or matches a schema.

Each AI execution must retain, as applicable:

Input/reference snapshot or a privacy-safe trace of it
Schema and validation version
Start/end time
Status and retry information
Token/cost/latency metadata where available
Validation results and error categories
Relevant output reference
Downstream domain operation
Logs must not unnecessarily retain raw sensitive candidate information or provider secrets.

The Admin Control Plane should manage supported configuration for:

49.3 Versioning and publication
Configuration must have a lifecycle that distinguishes draft, validated, published/active, and superseded or retired versions as appropriate.
Published configuration versions must be immutable historical records. A change creates a new version rather than rewriting the published version.
Each version must retain relevant metadata, including:
Version ID and configuration hash
Author and creation time
Validation result
Publisher and publication time
Previous version reference
Status
Rollback relationship where applicable
Audit event reference
Invalid configuration cannot become active.
49.4 Rollback
Rollback activates a previous valid configuration version through a new, auditable administrative action. It must not delete the intervening versions or rewrite historical executions.
A rollback must be validated against current compatibility and safety requirements. It cannot bypass authorization, data-protection requirements, or deterministic hard constraints.
49.5 Execution consistency
The runtime must retrieve and use the authoritative published configuration through the canonical server-side path. Local React state, localStorage, or an Admin UI value that is not consumed by actual execution is not authoritative.
Every AI execution must record the exact task version and relevant model/configuration version used. Source acquisition and scheduled execution must similarly retain the applicable connector and configuration context.

Every material administrative action must record:
Actor
Timestamp
Action
Target entity
Previous and new version or state where appropriate
Validation result
Reason or notes where appropriate
Outcome and error details where safe
Administrative configuration cannot override authentication, authorization, security, privacy boundaries, or deterministic hard-constraint enforcement.

50. Background processing, scheduler, and transactional reliability
JobPunter requires reliable background processing for acquisition, scheduled matching, JD retrieval, document parsing, AI execution, document generation, and notifications.
50.1 Initial execution model
The initial proposed design uses database-backed work items and a worker process. This avoids introducing a separate queue broker before a demonstrated need exists.
The scheduler creates durable work requests. Workers claim and execute work with controlled concurrency, retries, and observable state. The exact scheduler and worker deployment mechanism remains an implementation choice.
50.2 Work-item lifecycle
A work item should support states equivalent to:
Pending
Queued
Running
Succeeded
Partially succeeded
Retry scheduled
Failed
Cancelled
Each item should retain the operation type, relevant entity references, idempotency key where applicable, attempt count, creation and update times, next retry time, completion time, and safe error metadata.
The implementation must define how abandoned work is detected and recovered.
50.3 Transactional outbox
Where a database transaction must reliably trigger downstream work or external delivery, use a transactional outbox or an equivalent reliability pattern.
For example, if a transaction creates a durable domain event that requires a worker to act on it, the system must avoid a state where the domain record commits but the work request is silently lost.
The outbox must be processed idempotently. External delivery may be at-least-once, so downstream operations must tolerate retries and prevent unintended duplicate effects.
50.4 Infrastructure restraint
Do not introduce Redis, Kafka, or other separate messaging infrastructure merely by default. Add it only when measured throughput, concurrency, operational requirements, or deployment constraints justify the complexity.
Document Storage and Retention

Primary storage: Cloudflare R2 is JobPunter’s system-managed source of truth for document files. PostgreSQL stores document metadata and storage references.

Upload limit: Maximum 4 MB per file.

Retention: Default 30 days, configurable. Expiry must respect active workflow dependencies and must not silently remove documents still required by the system.

Lifecycle: Automated expiry and deletion, with usage monitoring, cost alerts, and auditable status.

External storage: Google Drive and other user-connected providers are optional secondary copies, never dependencies for JobPunter workspace access. External deletion or disconnection must not affect retained R2 files.

Security and integrity: Private, authorized access; time-limited download links; checksum verification; and secure handling of credentials.

Reliability: Uploads, retries, expiry, and deletion must handle partial failures without falsely reporting success. Missing files and failed cleanup must be detectable.

54. Error handling and operational observability
The system must distinguish user-correctable errors, transient external failures, permanent source restrictions, validation failures, authorization failures, and internal errors.
For each significant operation, the system should expose a safe and useful status to the user and retain sufficient operational information to diagnose failures.
Operational logs must:
Avoid secrets and unnecessary sensitive candidate data.
Include correlation identifiers where useful.
Preserve error categories and retry information.
Distinguish source errors from internal processing errors.
Support investigation of missing, duplicated, or incorrectly evaluated jobs.
Avoid presenting internal stack traces or secret-bearing provider responses to candidates.
The Admin Control Plane should expose operational status and relevant audit trails without giving administrators unnecessary access to private candidate content.

Firestore as the authoritative production application database → PostgreSQL is the target authoritative database.

A Node.js service layer as the target backend → Python/FastAPI is the target backend.
An implicitly fixed source list → sources are managed through an extensible registry and evaluated individually for feasibility and permitted access.
A search-first assumption that sources can be automated → acquisition capability and source state must be verified and truthfully reported.

61. New and revised architectural invariants
The v1.2 invariants remain in force except where explicitly superseded by the architecture changes above. The following v1.3 invariants are added.
INV-017 — Real Discovery: Production job discovery must represent real source records or user-supplied job content. Synthetic records must not be presented as real openings.
INV-018 — Source Extensibility: Job sources must be integrated through a common, extensible connector contract.
INV-019 — Source Truthfulness: Source access, operational state, and acquisition outcomes must reflect actual observed or configured conditions.
INV-020 — Database Authority: PostgreSQL is the authoritative production store for core application state under the v1.3 target architecture.
INV-021 — Server Authority: The backend enforces authorization, validation, and privileged business mutations; the frontend is not a trusted enforcement boundary.
INV-022 — Acquisition Convergence: Repeated acquisition of the same underlying vacancy must converge on the canonical job where identity can be established safely.
INV-023 — Acquisition Idempotency: Retried acquisition and processing must not create unintended duplicate jobs, application records, or material events.
INV-024 — Provider Independence: Core business logic must not depend on a specific AI provider, model, or provider SDK.
INV-025 — Deployment Independence: The production application must be deployable outside Google AI Studio.
INV-026 — Evidence Integrity: AI-generated candidate claims must be traceable to approved candidate evidence.
INV-027 — Configuration Governance: Published configuration versions are immutable, validated, auditable, and traceable to executions.
INV-028 — Candidate Isolation: Candidate-private records must remain isolated from other candidates.
INV-029 — Human Application Control: JobPunter must not claim that a real-world application or hiring event occurred unless recorded through an appropriate user action or verified integration.
INV-030 — No Silent Data Loss: Failed, partial, restricted, or unavailable acquisition and background operations must remain observable and recoverable where appropriate.
The implementation must retain the original v1.2 invariant register and its exact existing definitions. The numbering above extends it; it does not replace or renumber earlier invariants.

Lightweight Notification Specification
Purpose
Provide timely in-app and optional email alerts for important JobPunter events, prioritizing simplicity, reliability, and zero operating cost.
Requirements

Initial events: Notify users when job discovery completes, a new Punt is available, an evaluation requires attention, or a tracked application changes status. Preserve existing support-ticket notification requirements.
Processing: Generate notifications from actual persisted events or results. Reuse the existing backend worker and outbox mechanisms where available. Prevent duplicate notifications when an event is processed more than once.

Existing notification functionality remains intact.

JOBPUNTER ENGINEERING MISSION
The Battering Ram Doctrine | Engineering Ground Rules
1. The Mission
Build JobPunter to win.
We are building a competitive product in a hostile environment. External platforms protect their interests. Competitors fight for market share. Infrastructure fails. Access changes. Dependencies become unreliable. The application must be engineered to withstand these realities and continue delivering value.
JobPunter's interests come first. Our engineering exists to advance those interests.
We will pursue maximum useful capability, exploit available opportunities, overcome obstacles, and build a product that does not depend on the goodwill of external parties to remain effective.
We do not build to participate. We build to compete, survive, and win.
2. The Battering Ram Doctrine
2.1. Never surrender capability unnecessarily.
A restriction, blocked request, failed integration, or technical limitation is an obstacle to overcome, not an automatic reason to retreat. Investigate every viable route. Find alternatives. Engineer around dependencies. Keep pushing until the strongest practical solution has been exhausted.
2.2. Exploit every legitimate advantage.
Look for gaps in coverage, weaknesses in competing workflows, overlooked acquisition channels, alternative data paths, architectural advantages, and opportunities others have neglected. Do not assume the obvious route is the only route. Do not let another party's preferred way of doing business dictate the limits of our product.
2.3. Resilience is a weapon.
Build for adversity, not ideal conditions. Redundancy, independent acquisition paths, replaceable dependencies, automatic recovery, graceful degradation, and operational visibility are strategic advantages. When one route closes, others must remain available. When a component fails, the rest of the application must keep delivering wherever possible.
2.4. Enforce the mission in every line of code.
Every abstraction, condition, permission check, dependency, fallback, and failure path must justify its existence by the value it provides. No blanket restriction, defensive mechanism, or architectural convention gets unquestioned authority to disable useful functionality. Controls must address actual risks without unnecessarily sacrificing capability.
2.5. Be relentless about outcomes.
Code that exists but cannot deliver its intended capability is not success. A green test suite cannot substitute for a working product. A component that reports its own failure accurately is still a failed product component if it cannot perform its job. We measure what JobPunter accomplishes, not how respectable its engineering paperwork looks.
2.6. Protect our interests without sabotaging our future.
Aggression is not recklessness. Avoidable security vulnerabilities, fragile hacks, uncontrolled costs, and dependencies that threaten the entire product are weaknesses. Engineer solutions that preserve competitive advantage, protect the platform, and remain effective as conditions change.
3. One Doctrine. The Entire Application.
These rules govern the Admin Backend, Candidate Backend, frontend, integrations, data architecture, infrastructure, and every subsequent component.
No repository is exempt. No component is considered successful merely because it was declared complete. No architectural boundary is an excuse for a broken user journey.

Infrastructure: Sustain performance, availability, and recovery under adverse conditions.
Security: Protect the product and its users through effective, targeted controls.
Testing: Prove that intended capabilities work through real execution paths.
The objective is a formidable application, not a collection of individually respectable components.
4. The Dogged Enforcer Standard
Engineering does not stop at identifying a problem, documenting a limitation, or making a failure easier to observe.
We pursue the intended outcome. We investigate until the obstacle is understood. We evaluate alternatives until a viable route emerges or the remaining constraint is established by evidence. We implement, test, and verify. If the result falls short, we continue.
No invented progress. No cosmetic completion. No retreat into endless audits. No declaring victory because CI is green while the product remains incapable.
Every claim must be backed by evidence. Every material limitation must be visible. Every engineering decision that sacrifices capability must have a concrete justification and the strongest available alternative must be considered.
Execution must remain disciplined: one repository at a time, existing work preserved, and consequential Git operations performed only with explicit authorisation. The battering ram is directed at engineering obstacles, not at change control or the integrity of the codebase.

JobPunter must be difficult to disable, difficult to constrain, adaptable under pressure, and relentlessly focused on delivering value.

Admin & System Control Plane
JobPunter retains a distinct, protected Admin & System Control Plane. It governs supported system behavior through persisted, validated configuration. It does not dynamically rewrite application code.
49.1 Separation and authorization
The Admin Control Plane must have:
A protected /admin route or equivalent boundary
A dedicated administrative application shell and navigation
Server-enforced administrative authorization
Explicit role/session state
Separation from candidate navigation and workflow state
Auditable administrative actions
Least-privilege access to sensitive configuration and operational information
Sharing visual components with the candidate workspace is acceptable. Sharing a frontend shell or hiding a navigation item is not a substitute for authorization.
49.2 Configuration management
The Admin Control Plane should manage supported configuration for:
AI tasks and task versions
AI providers and models
Task-to-model assignments and fallback rules
Matching thresholds and supported decision settings
Job-source registry entries and connector configuration
Schedules and acquisition controls
Integration configuration and observed health
Notifications and other implemented operational settings
Only capabilities implemented and validated in application code may be controlled through configuration.
49.3 Versioning and publication
Configuration must have a lifecycle that distinguishes draft, validated, published/active, and superseded or retired versions as appropriate.
Published configuration versions must be immutable historical records. A change creates a new version rather than rewriting the published version.
Each version must retain relevant metadata, including:
Version ID and configuration hash
Author and creation time
Validation result
Publisher and publication time
Previous version reference
Status
Rollback relationship where applicable
Audit event reference
Invalid configuration cannot become active.
49.4 Rollback
Rollback activates a previous valid configuration version through a new, auditable administrative action. It must not delete the intervening versions or rewrite historical executions.
A rollback must be validated against current compatibility and safety requirements. It cannot bypass authorization, data-protection requirements, or deterministic hard constraints.
49.5 Execution consistency
The runtime must retrieve and use the authoritative published configuration through the canonical server-side path. Local React state, localStorage, or an Admin UI value that is not consumed by actual execution is not authoritative.
Every AI execution must record the exact task version and relevant model/configuration version used. Source acquisition and scheduled execution must similarly retain the applicable connector and configuration context.
49.6 Administrative audit
Every material administrative action must record:
Actor
Timestamp
Action
Target entity
Previous and new version or state where appropriate
Validation result
Reason or notes where appropriate
Outcome and error details where safe
Administrative configuration cannot override authentication, authorization, security, privacy boundaries, or deterministic hard-constraint enforcement.

Search: Find tickets by candidate or relevant ticket details.
Filters: Filter tickets by status, unread state, date and assigned administrator, where supported.
Candidate context: Display relevant candidate information alongside the ticket, with links to User Records.
Ticket assignment: Allow tickets to be assigned or reassigned to support administrators.
Admin replies: Allow authorized administrators to respond directly to candidates within the ticket thread.
Impersonation handoff: Navigate from a support ticket to the candidate's record and initiate an authorized impersonation session for troubleshooting.
Timestamps: Display message timestamps and ticket creation and update times.
Audit trail: Record relevant support actions, including ticket creation, replies, assignments, status changes, closure, linked follow-up tickets and access where required.
Privacy and access control: Ensure candidates can access only their own tickets and administrators can access only tickets they are authorized to handle.
Notification: Every new ticket must send an email and in-app notification to admin and user. Every resolved and closed ticket must also do the same.
Recommended MVP
Start with ticket creation, titles and issue details, persistent two-way text messaging, conversation history, message delivery states, unread indicators, open/resolved status, linked follow-up tickets, basic search and filters, and audit logging.
Add ticket assignment if multiple administrators will handle support.

AI cannot create employers, responsibilities, achievements, metrics, credentials, technologies or outcomes.
INV-003 — JD Content
Actual usable job-description content is authoritative for job evaluation.
A title, subject line, preview card or email heading cannot substitute for the JD.
INV-004 — Incomplete JD
If usable JD content is unavailable or materially incomplete:
INCOMPLETE / REQUIRES REVIEW
The system must not fabricate missing content.
INV-005 — Canonical Job
One underlying vacancy must resolve to one canonical Job record.
INV-006 — Candidate Isolation
Private candidate evaluations, evidence and applications must remain isolated.
INV-007 — Deterministic Authority
AI cannot override deterministic hard blockers.
INV-008 — Human Application Control
The system may recommend and prepare.
The candidate approves and submits.
INV-009 — Admin Authority
Admin UI is never the security boundary.
Server-side authorization is.
INV-010 — Configuration Authority
Published Firestore configuration is authoritative.
Frontend local state is not.
INV-011 — AI Traceability
Every material AI result must identify the exact task/configuration version, provider and model used.
INV-012 — Historical Integrity
Changing current configuration must not rewrite historical execution or application-package provenance.
INV-013 — Real Jobs
Production JobPunter cannot present hypothetical/demo jobs as discovered opportunities.
INV-014 — Unknown Means Unknown
Unknown salary, location, authorization, requirement, freshness or application route remains unknown until verified.
INV-015 — Integration Honesty
A UI must never state that an integration is connected, operational or verified unless the system actually established that state.
INV-016 — Security Cannot Be Configured Away
No prompt, admin setting, feature flag or AI model can disable security or deterministic enforcement.

Do not continue adding major candidate-facing features yet.
The current implementation has enough functional surface area.
The next work should be architecture closure, beginning with:

32. Current Audit Conclusion
JobPunter is not a failed implementation.
It has substantial working product logic and a surprisingly broad prototype surface.
However, it currently has a significant gap between:
what the UI represents
and:
what the underlying architecture actually guarantees.
The largest risk is not missing screens.
The largest risk is false confidence:
a restricted-looking Admin panel without proven authorization
a Firestore connection without Firestore being authoritative
AI governance UI without guaranteed execution governance
versioning UI without durable immutable configuration history
Google integration UI without complete service authorization architecture
job discovery logic without verified real-source ingestion
structured AI output without complete semantic validation
Job Pool terminology without a canonical shared Job repository
demo discovery data inside the product workflow
The next phase therefore moves JobPunter from:
feature-rich prototype
toward:
secure, evidence-grounded, auditable application architecture.
The PRD's central architectural requirement remains unchanged: the existing AI Studio project should be audited and selectively reused rather than blindly extended or discarded.

JobPunter Domain Model v1.1
Status: Proposed for relational-model translation Predecessor: Domain Model v1.0 Purpose: Define the stable business/domain concepts of JobPunter before database schema and API implementation.

1. Core Product Model
JobPunter operates around four distinct concepts:
DISCOVERY
   ↓
JOB
   ↓
EVALUATION
   ↓
PUNT
   ↓
APPLICATION
These concepts must not be collapsed into one another.
Job
A Job is a canonical, real-world employment opportunity known to JobPunter.
A Job exists independently of any particular candidate.
Evaluation
An Evaluation is JobPunter's candidate-specific assessment of a Job against:
candidate evidence
candidate preferences
target role
hard constraints
job requirements
deterministic rules
AI interpretation where applicable
An Evaluation is private to the candidate.
Punt
A Punt is a Job opportunity that has been determined to deserve application effort.
Punt = a worthwhile application opportunity.
Punt is therefore a positive qualification of an opportunity, not a rejection, skip, withdrawal, or application status.
The product metric:
48 jobs discovered, 10 real punts
is therefore a valid core JobPunter concept.
A Punt does not mean the candidate has applied.
Job discovered
      ↓
Evaluated
      ↓
Punt
      ↓
Candidate chooses whether/how to apply
      ↓
Application
The candidate remains in control.
Implementation principle: Punt is a domain concept. The product may use different presentation terminology in the future without requiring the underlying domain model to adopt that terminology as a database or API primitive.

2. Identity & Access
2.1 User
Represents the JobPunter account holder.
Responsibilities:
account identity
account lifecycle
authorization context
ownership boundary
A User does not directly own shared Jobs.
Account lifecycle
Conceptually:
ACTIVE
SUSPENDED
DEACTIVATED
DELETION_REQUESTED
DELETION_PROCESSING
DELETED / ANONYMIZED
Exact retention/deletion semantics are defined separately.

2.2 Identity
Represents an authentication identity associated with a User.
Examples:
Google identity
future authentication provider
A User may have multiple Identities.

3. Candidate Intelligence
3.1 CandidateProfile
The candidate's authoritative professional profile.
One User has one active Master Career Profile.
The profile is composed from structured candidate information and approved evidence.

3.2 Experience
A professional experience record.
Examples:
employment
contract engagement
leadership role
Contains structured facts plus descriptive information.

3.3 Education
A formal educational qualification.
Examples:
degree
diploma
postgraduate qualification

3.4 Project
A candidate project that can demonstrate capability.
Projects may be:
professional
personal
portfolio
entrepreneurial
Projects can provide evidence supporting evaluation and application claims.

3.5 Achievement
A measurable or otherwise meaningful accomplishment.
Examples:
delivery result
business outcome
process improvement
recognition

3.6 Certification
A formal certification awarded by a recognized body.

3.7 Credential
A broader verified qualification or credential that does not necessarily fit the certification model.
This distinction allows the profile to represent qualifications without forcing every credential into one category.

3.8 Skill
A structured candidate capability.
Skills may eventually carry:
category
proficiency
evidence
recency
source
A Skill is not authoritative merely because it was entered or inferred. Its evidentiary basis matters.

4. Candidate Claims & Evidence
The previous model treated Evidence as the primary object. v1.1 makes the semantic relationship more explicit.
4.1 CandidateClaim
A claim about the candidate that JobPunter may use in evaluation or application generation.
Examples:
"Managed a team of 8 engineers."

"Reduced deployment time by 40%."

"Built and launched a video conferencing SaaS platform."
A claim is not automatically authoritative.

4.2 Evidence
Evidence supports a CandidateClaim or structured candidate fact.
Evidence has lifecycle:
DRAFT
PENDING_REVIEW
APPROVED
REJECTED
ARCHIVED
Only approved evidence may support authoritative candidate claims.
AI may discover or propose evidence candidates, but AI cannot make them authoritative.

4.3 EvidenceSource
Records where evidence came from.
Examples:
user entry
uploaded document
portfolio
external import
system extraction
The provenance chain should ultimately be:
Candidate Claim
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Evidence Source
Evidence may also carry temporal validity:
effective_from
effective_to
verified_at
expires_at
where applicable.

5. Candidate Preferences
CandidatePreferences
Represents the candidate's constraints and preferences for job evaluation.
Examples:
employment type
location
remote preference
salary expectations
role preferences
exclusions
Preferences are distinct from hard system eligibility rules.

6. target roles
TargetRole
A candidate-specific pursuit configuration.
A candidate may maintain multiple pathways.
Examples conceptually:
Technical Product Management
Product Operations
Technical Program Management
A pathway can define:
target roles
priorities
acceptable variations
configuration
evaluation context
Evaluations are performed against a particular pathway.

7. Job Intelligence
7.1 Source
Represents a known external job source.
Examples:
job board
employer career site
marketplace
ATS
feed
A Source is not itself an acquisition mechanism.

7.2 SourceConnector
The mechanism used to acquire information from a Source.
Examples:
API
scraper
browser automation
feed
email ingestion
manual submission
This distinction is important because one Source may eventually support multiple acquisition mechanisms.
Connector configuration should include things such as:
connector type
version
capabilities
limits
health
authentication requirements

7.3 AcquisitionRun
A particular execution of acquisition against a Source/Connector.
Tracks:
start/end
status
metrics
errors
idempotency

7.4 RawSourceRecord
The externally acquired record before canonical normalization.
It preserves the source observation.
A RawSourceRecord may eventually resolve to a Job.

7.5 JobResolution
Determines how a RawSourceRecord relates to a canonical Job.
Resolution operations may include:
new resolution
match
supersede
merge
split
reject
This provides the history necessary to handle changing or conflicting source identities.

8. Canonical Job
Job
The canonical representation of a real-world vacancy.
A Job belongs to the shared Job Pool, not to an individual candidate.
Core characteristics include:
canonical title
employer
location
employment type
compensation
application route
lifecycle state
freshness
current JD
provenance
Job lifecycle
Separate from freshness:
DISCOVERED
ACTIVE
CLOSED
EXPIRED
UNKNOWN
Freshness
Separate state:
FRESH
STALE
UNKNOWN
A Job can therefore be:
ACTIVE + STALE
without falsely implying that the vacancy is closed.

9. Job Provenance
JobProvenance
Records observations connecting a canonical Job to external sources.
The system must be able to answer:
Where did we get this job, when did we observe it, and what source record supported it?
One Job may have many provenance records.

10. Job Description
JobDescriptionVersion
A versioned representation of the Job Description associated with a Job.
A Job may have multiple JD versions.
This is necessary because the source JD can change.
Each version retains:
content
content hash
source record
retrieval time
completeness status

JobRequirement
A structured requirement extracted from a particular JD version.
Examples:
required skill
experience requirement
education requirement
location requirement
authorization requirement
responsibility
preferred qualification
Requirements belong to a specific JD version.

11. Discovery
DiscoveryRequest
Represents an intentional request for job discovery.
This is different from an AcquisitionRun.
For example:
Candidate requests:
"Find technical PM jobs in Nigeria."

        ↓

DiscoveryRequest

        ↓

Multiple acquisition operations

        ↓

Jobs
This allows JobPunter to distinguish:
what discovery was requested
how acquisition was executed
what Jobs were ultimately discovered

12. Evaluation
Evaluation
A candidate-specific assessment of a Job for a particular TargetRole.
An Evaluation must identify the exact context under which it was produced.
At minimum:
Candidate state
+
Preferences
+
target role
+
Job version
+
JD version
+
RuleSet version
+
AI configuration/version where applicable
The evaluation therefore represents a historical assessment, not a permanently current truth.
Evaluation lifecycle
REQUESTED
RUNNING
COMPLETED
FAILED
DEGRADED
REQUIRES_REVIEW

12.1 EvaluationRequirementResult
Assessment of an individual JobRequirement against the candidate.
Contains:
assessment
confidence
explanation

12.2 EvaluationEvidence
Links a requirement assessment to the evidence supporting it.
This allows the system to distinguish:
direct evidence
transferable evidence
insufficient evidence
conflicting evidence

12.3 EvaluationFeedback
Candidate feedback about an evaluation.
Examples:
helpful
incorrect fit
missed evidence
incorrect blocker
incorrect interpretation
Feedback does not mutate the historical evaluation.

13. Deterministic Rules
RuleSet
A named collection of deterministic business rules.
Examples:
eligibility rules
hard constraints
matching rules
decision policies

RuleSetVersion
Immutable version of a RuleSet.
Evaluations reference the exact version used.
This gives us the same historical reproducibility principle already established for AI configuration.

14. Punt
Punt
A candidate-specific qualification that identifies a canonical Job as worth application effort.
A Punt should preserve the relationship between:
Candidate
Job
Evaluation
TargetRole
A Punt is therefore not:
a Job state
an Application state
a rejection
a withdrawal
an employer event
an AI recommendation
a generic candidate disposition
Critical distinction
An Evaluation may say:
System recommendation: APPLY
That does not automatically mean:
Punt
A Punt represents the product's qualifying opportunity concept.
The precise rule for when an Evaluation becomes a Punt must be defined in the business invariants before schema implementation.
This is one of the items we should explicitly settle during the relational/invariant pass rather than hiding the decision in implementation.

15. Applications
Application
Represents one candidate application attempt against one canonical Job.
A Job can exist without an Application.
A candidate may have multiple historical attempts against the same Job, but only one active attempt at a time.
Application state represents the candidate-visible application lifecycle, not the employer's internal recruitment workflow.
Candidate-facing lifecycle
PLANNED
   ↓
APPROVED
   ↓
SUBMITTED
   ↓
INTERVIEW
   ↓
OFFER
   ↓
ACCEPTED
Exit states may include:
REJECTED
WITHDRAWN
CLOSED
SCREENING is explicitly excluded from the application lifecycle.
Employer-side screening may be recorded as an event if useful, but it is not a candidate-facing Application state.

16. Application Events
ApplicationEvent
Immutable historical record of material application activity.
Examples:
application approved
application submitted
interview recorded
offer recorded
candidate withdrawal
employer rejection
The system must not fabricate real-world events.
Every externally asserted event should retain its actor/source and, where appropriate, submission evidence.

17. Application Package
ApplicationPackage
A versioned collection of materials prepared for a specific Application.
It retains the historical context used to generate it.
Examples:
Job snapshot
JD version
profile snapshot
evidence snapshot
evaluation
CV
cover letter
Q&A
Regeneration creates a new version.
It does not overwrite the historical package.

ApplicationPackageItem
An individual artifact or structured item within the package.
May reference:
Document
generated content
evidence references

18. Documents
Document
Metadata describing a stored candidate document.
Binary content belongs in object storage.
PostgreSQL stores:
identity
type
filename
MIME type
storage reference
checksum
metadata

19. AI Governance
AITask
Defines an AI capability.
Examples:

AITaskVersion
Immutable published configuration of an AI task.
Contains:

Published versions cannot be modified.

It identifies:

25. Core Invariants Carried Forward
The v1.1 model preserves the fundamental rules:
One real vacancy → one canonical Job.
Jobs belong to the shared Job Pool, not to candidates.
Candidate evaluations are private.
Candidate evidence is authoritative for candidate claims.
AI cannot manufacture authoritative candidate evidence.
Hard constraints cannot be overridden by AI.
Job lifecycle and freshness are separate.
Historical evaluations must be reproducible from their recorded inputs/configuration.
Published AI configuration is immutable.
RuleSet versions are immutable.
Application history is append-only.
Application packages are historical snapshots.
The system cannot fabricate real-world application events.
Human control remains required for real-world application submission.
Repeated processing must converge without duplicate domain records.
Candidate-private information never enters the shared Job Pool.
Unknown remains unknown. The system does not infer certainty merely to fill a field.
A Punt is a qualifying opportunity worth application effort, not a rejection.
A Punt is distinct from an Application.
Screening is not a candidate-facing Application state.

The next step after this
I would not yet jump into SQLAlchemy tables.
There are several decisions in this v1.1 that need to become mathematically precise first, particularly:
Punt qualification → Evaluation → Candidate action → Application
and then:
Job identity → resolution → merge/split → canonical Job
and:
Evaluation validity → material changes → invalidation/re-evaluation

20. Invariant tests
Critical invariants should have automated tests.
Examples:
A user cannot read another user's Evaluation.

A user cannot alter another user's Application.

A Job cannot be owned by a candidate.

A Punt cannot become REJECTED.

An AI result cannot override a hard constraint.

An Application cannot jump from PLANNED to ACCEPTED.

SCREENING cannot become an Application state.

A submitted application cannot be fabricated by a UI-only action.

An old Evaluation cannot be mutated into a new evaluation.

Two active Applications cannot exist for the same candidate + Job.

A duplicate acquisition cannot create duplicate canonical Jobs.

A stale evaluation cannot silently remain CURRENT after a material input change.
These are not merely documentation. They become executable safeguards.

JobPunter — Brief Brand Specification
Status: Working brand direction / v0.1 Purpose: Establish the core visual and verbal direction before detailed identity development.

1. Brand
Name: JobPunter
Core idea: JobPunter helps people make smarter decisions about which job opportunities deserve their application effort.
The brand should feel like a confident, intelligent job-hunting product, not a conventional employment portal.
Brand personality
Confident
Sharp
Practical
Energetic
Slightly irreverent
Modern
Human
Trustworthy
Positioning: Not another job board. Not generic career coaching. Not an AI gimmick.

2. Brand Vocabulary
The product has its own deliberately limited vocabulary.
Job Pool
The broader pool of job opportunities available to the user.
Punt
A job opportunity that JobPunter considers worth the candidate's application effort.
A Punt is therefore an opportunity worth taking a calculated shot at, based on the product's assessment.
Applied
A Punt for which the user has actually submitted an application.
Landed
A successful job outcome.
Core progression
Job Pool → Punt → Apply → Land
Hunt is explicitly excluded from the brand vocabulary.

3. Logo Direction
Primary concept: JP monogram
The logo is a fusion of J and P, rather than two separate letters.

4. Wordmark
Primary wordmark
jobpunter
Entirely lowercase.
The lowercase treatment is intentional and should remain intact.
Colour treatment
job → White punter → Jobpunter Yellow
This visually distinguishes the two components of the name without introducing capitalisation.

5. Core Colour System
The identity deliberately uses a restricted core palette.
Primary
Black #000000 or a carefully selected near-black
Primary brand canvas and dominant visual field.
Signature
JobPunter Yellow #E8FF00
This is the signature brand colour and should eventually become strongly associated with Punt.
Contrast
White #FFFFFF
Used for:
Wordmark
J element
Primary text on black
Contrast
Light surfaces where required

6. Supporting UI Colours
The core brand should remain black/yellow/white. Supporting neutrals are permitted for interface hierarchy.
Dark Grey — cards, panels and secondary dark surfaces
Mid Grey — secondary text and metadata
Light Grey — borders and dividers
Off-white — optional light-mode/background surface
Exact values to be defined during UI design.

7. Functional Colours
Functional colours are not additional brand colours.
They exist purely to communicate product state.
Green → success / positive outcome / landed
Red → rejection / error / negative state
Blue → informational state where genuinely necessary
Yellow → Punt / important JobPunter-specific emphasis
Key principle
Yellow belongs to JobPunter.
Green, red and blue belong to the interface's functional language.

8. Colour Philosophy
The identity should be high contrast, restrained and immediately recognisable.
Preferred
Black + yellow + white
The brand should remain recognisable even when reduced to a simple yellow JP mark on black.

9. Visual Character
The visual system should feel:
Bold, clean, intelligent and slightly rebellious.
It should have enough attitude to distinguish JobPunter from conventional job platforms while maintaining enough restraint to feel like a serious product.
The identity should communicate:
Judgement over volume. Strategy over random applications. Confidence over desperation.

10. Design Principle
The brand should not merely say JobPunter.
The product should behave like JobPunter.
For example:
43 jobs in your Job Pool 10 Punts worth your attention
The yellow then becomes more than a colour. It becomes a visual signal for the product's central concept:
This is a Punt. This deserves your attention.

APP UIWorking UI Design Direction
Status: Working decisions, not yet a finalized visual system.
This document records the current direction for JobPunter’s user interface. It guides design exploration and implementation while leaving unapproved visual details open.
1. Core brand palette
Black: #000000
Yellow: #E8FF00
White: #FFFFFF

Black, signature yellow and white define the brand. Charcoal and grey support the interface without competing with the core palette.
2. Canvas and surfaces
Main canvas
Near-black · #050505
Standard card
Charcoal · #111111
Use a near-black canvas with charcoal cards, white primary text, secondary grey text and restrained borders. Avoid large expanses of mid-grey or off-white that could dilute the brand.
3. Punt visual treatment
Yellow’s strongest meaning is reserved for Punt: an opportunity judged worth the candidate’s application effort.
Use selective yellow markers, labels, edges or borders to identify Punt opportunities.
Use a full-yellow featured card occasionally, not as the default for every job card. Use black text on yellow.
Keep yellow emphasis purposeful. It signals product meaning rather than acting as general decoration.
Functional status colours may be used for actual states, but must not compete with yellow’s association with Punt.
4. Card geometry
Use crisp rectangles, thin borders and modest corner rounding. Avoid excessive pill shapes, oversized radii, heavy shadows and decorative depth. The interface should feel precise, confident and practical.
5. Information density
Use balanced density: enough relevant information on a job card for a candidate to assess suitability and likely value without opening the job. Keep secondary details subordinate and the card easy to scan.
6. Design principle
Judgement over volume.
Strategy over random applications.
Confidence over desperation.
The interface should help candidates distinguish opportunities worth pursuing, not simply present an endless wall of listings. Create hierarchy through typography, spacing, selective emphasis and useful information, rather than making every card look the same.
7. Decisions recorded
Canvas: Near-black background, charcoal cards, white text and yellow accents.
Punt treatment: Selective yellow markers and borders, with occasional full-yellow featured cards.
Geometry: Crisp rectangles, thin borders and modest rounding.
Information density: Balanced; enough detail to assess a job without opening it.
Yellow’s meaning: Strongest association reserved for Punt.
